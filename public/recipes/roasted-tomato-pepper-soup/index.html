Roasted Tomato and Red Pepper Soup

Roasted tomatoes and peppers blend into a smooth, bright soup with basil and a touch of cream.

Written by Elba Adams · Mexico

Total time: 45 minutesYield: 4 servings
Jump to recipe

About this recipe

Roasting gives tomatoes and peppers a deeper, sweeter flavor than a quick stovetop simmer. Their edges caramelize in the oven while onion and garlic soften alongside them. After blending, the soup tastes rich and balanced without needing a long list of ingredients or a complicated stock.

This recipe is especially useful when tomatoes are plentiful or starting to soften. Ripe tomatoes produce the best flavor; if yours are very tart, taste before adding a small pinch of sugar. Keep the soup thick for dipping grilled cheese, or loosen it with extra broth for a lighter bowl. A swirl of cream is optional.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb (900 g) ripe tomatoes, halved
  • 2 red bell peppers, seeded and quartered
  • 1 medium onion, cut into wedges
  • 4 garlic cloves, peeled
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • 2 cups vegetable broth
  • 1/4 cup fresh basil leaves
  • 2 tablespoons cream (optional)
  • Black pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Heat oven to 425°F (220°C). Spread tomatoes, peppers, onion and garlic on a large rimmed sheet pan. Toss with oil and salt, placing the tomato cut sides up.
  2. Roast 25 to 30 minutes until the vegetables soften and show browned edges. Let cool briefly, then carefully transfer vegetables and their juices to a blender.
  3. Add broth and basil. Blend until smooth, working in batches if necessary and venting the lid so steam can escape. Transfer to a saucepan and simmer gently for 5 minutes.
  4. Taste and add pepper or more salt. Stir in cream if using. Serve with basil and toasted bread.
Storage & helpful tip
Refrigerate in a covered container for up to 4 days, or freeze without cream for up to 2 months. Reheat gently before stirring in cream.

Final thoughts

A pan of roasted vegetables becomes a comforting soup with very little work at the stove. It is easy to make ahead for lunch and pairs well with toast, a sandwich or a simple salad. Adjust the thickness to suit the meal and finish each bowl just before serving.

Helpful cooking notes

  • Choose the tomatoes. Very juicy tomatoes can make a thinner soup; simmer uncovered to reduce it.
  • Blend safely. Do not fill a blender with hot soup to the top. Blend in batches with a vented lid.
  • For a dairy-free bowl. Skip the cream or finish with a drizzle of olive oil.
